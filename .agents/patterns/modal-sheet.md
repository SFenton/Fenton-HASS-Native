# Modal sheet

> **What:** how web hash modals become native sheets. **Read when:** presenting a modal.

- Web hash routes (`#lights-overview`) map to a `HomeSheet` enum driving `.sheet(item:)`. The URL hash is not needed natively.
- Container: `HassDesign/HassModal.swift`. New modals use `HassModal(title, closeIdentifier:) { content }`; existing ones put `SheetCloseItem { dismiss() }` in their toolbar and `modalChrome()` on their root.
- Close: the circle-X `SheetCloseItem` at the top trailing edge (`Button(role: .close)` on iOS 26), never a text "Done" or "Close" (owner, 2026-10-13, matching FestivalNativeApps; HIG Toolbars: "Close dismisses a modal; prefer their standard symbols without text labels"). A sheet that saves keeps its Save or Add button; Cancel stays only where it discards an edit.
- Create and edit sheets (direction G, Sheets): `SheetCancelItem` (circle x, leading) and `SheetConfirmItem("Save", isEnabled:isBusy:)` (a check on a filled `#0071E3` circle, trailing, with an inline `ArcSpinner` while saving), both in `HassDesign/SheetEditToolbar.swift`, as in Calendar and Reminders. Never text Cancel/Save buttons and never a second copy of these. **The check is visible and disabled until there is a change** (owner, 2026-10-16; `SheetEditState.canConfirm(isValid:hasChanges:isBusy:)`): a new item needs a valid draft, an edit also needs a difference from what is saved, and a save in flight shows the spinner. It is dimmed when disabled and filled `#0071E3` when enabled; never hide it. Sheets still on their own text Save/Submit buttons (the humidifier schedule editor, the feedback form, the bed and wake-light editors' in-page buttons) are known gaps.
- Header: no translucent band; content fades out under it (`modalChrome()`, [header-edge-fade](header-edge-fade.md)). Each page pushed inside a modal applies `modalChrome()` too, and so does every sheet that builds its own `NavigationStack` (`FilterSheet` did not until 2026-10-16, which left the system's translucent bar behind "Filter Recipes"). Modals have no house backdrop ([page-backdrop](page-backdrop.md)).
- **Height (owner, 2026-10-16; replaces 2026-10-13's "all modals full height on iPhone").** `.hassSheet()` is **content-fitted** by default: `modalChrome()` reports the page's scroll content height (`onScrollGeometryChange`: content plus the header and bottom insets, from the page's own vertical scroller, iOS 18 and later; HassDesign `SheetFit.swift`) to the sheet, which sets a `.height` detent capped at large (`SheetFit.detent`), with the grabber visible. It opens at `.medium` until the first measurement, at the last measured height after that (remembered per call site by `fitKey`, default `#fileID:#line`), and moves only for changes of 2 pt or more. The first page to report owns the height, so pushing a page inside the sheet does not resize it. On iPad the form or page sheet is fitted to the same height (`presentationSizing(.form.fitted(...))` with the measured ideal height). `.hassSheet(_, height: .full)` opens at large and is **required** for: a remote (media remote), any modal with a section tab bar, recipe detail (full screen), a sheet that pushes pages (Account, Quick Links, room card, room overview, Sprinklers, the presence override) and live video or a gradient (camera viewer, Weather, the scanner). A new sheet states its height in review: fitted unless one of those applies. `FilterSheet(height:)` follows the same rule and keys its memory by title.
- **iPad size (plan Phase 4, 2026-10-15).** Every sheet names it: `.hassSheet(.page)` for viewing and control sheets with tile grids, dials, maps or a second column (chip sheets, Security System, Weather, Quick Links, room card sheets, Sprinklers, Custom Light, remotes, thermostat, vacuum, recipe detail, camera); `.hassSheet(.form)` for lists, editors and prompts (light mode picker, Vacation and Solo Trip editors, Recipes filters, Bed temperature scope, Add Item, todo editors, filter and sort sheets). `HassSheetWidth` has two cases and the default is `.form`: there is no `.automatic`, so a sheet that forgets still gets a narrow readable sheet instead of the system's default, and the rule above decides when to ask for `.page`. `hassSheetSizing(_:)` stays for a sheet that must manage its own detents; none does today. A sheet that bypasses `hassSheet` for its own reason (the item editor's grabber that hides while a save blocks dismissal) still routes through it and overrides the one modifier after.
- Appearance (owner decision, 2026-10-15): pages stay dark on the wallpaper, sheets follow the system appearance. `hassSheet()` and `HassModal` apply `followsSystemAppearance()` (the scheme `RootView` publishes as `systemColorScheme`, because the shell's `preferredColorScheme(.dark)` hides it from the environment); a sheet over a photo or gradient says `hassSheet(appearance: .dark)` (Weather, the camera viewer). Never write `.preferredColorScheme` in a feature sheet otherwise, and give every sheet `hassSheet()` or `HassModal`, or it stays dark. Colour sheet content only with the adaptive tokens (`Ink.*`, `Surface.sheetGround/sheetCell/secondaryInSheet/separator`, `ColorFamily.glyph`): no `.white`, `Color.black`, `Palette.*` or `Surface.secondaryOnModule` on a view a sheet shows, except text on a fixed fill. A journey renders sheets in light with `FENTON_APPEARANCE=light` (`launchEnvironment`, Debug builds) and audits them with `FentonHomeApp.audit`.
- A sheet that navigates (room → lights) pushes inside its own `NavigationStack` with Back, matching the web's in-sheet back.
- A tabbed sheet (media remote, thermostat, vacuum, recipe detail) uses `HassUI/Common/ModalTabBar.swift`, pinned along the bottom with `.modalTabBar(...)` after `modalChrome()` (direction G; `placement: .top` remains for a sheet whose lane has not moved yet): a system segmented control on a glass capsule (`navigationGlass`: Liquid Glass `glassEffect` on iOS 26, a thin-material capsule before). On **iPhone and the folded Duo (compact width) it shows the web's icons** (owner, 2026-10-16: "show icons like the web app instead of text titles"), each named for VoiceOver by its title; **iPad and the unfolded Duo (regular width) show the titles**; accessibility sizes show a menu. The icons are the web's MDI names mapped through `SymbolMap` (`HassUI/Common/ModalTabSymbols.swift`, citing `ha-sfenton-react-dash@9ac2a46:src/constants/surfaceSemantics.ts`); every tabbed sheet passes `symbol:`. `ModalTabBarStyle.form(hasSymbols:compactWidth:accessibilitySize:)` decides. Never a second tab control.
- Why a segmented control on glass at the bottom (direction G, owner-approved mockups, 2026-10-13): it stays a segmented control, not a tab bar, because HIG Segmented controls (iOS, iPadOS) say "Consider it for switching between closely related subviews; use a tab bar for completely separate app sections", and a modal's sections are related views of one device. The glass places it on the navigation layer floating over the content, like the web's bottom `ModalIconTabNav` (HIG: Materials, Liquid Glass). Segments hold "text or images, not a mix", about 5 on iPhone. To change a tab's content without a hard cut, drive the panel with `HassUI/Common/SmoothContentSwap.swift` (fade out, swap while hidden, fade in; Reduce Motion swaps at once); the vacuum sheet does.
