# Page backdrop

> **What:** the blurred photo of the house behind every page. **Read when:** a page sets its own background, or you touch `pageChrome()`.

Owner, 2026-10-13: "No background image (blurred) of the house like web? Image should also be consistent across pages. (Modals do not have background)". Web: `ha-sfenton-react-dash@9ac2a46:src/components/shell/AppShell.module.css` `.backdrop` and `.scrim`, the image Home Assistant serves at `/local/images/house.webp`.

## Rules

- **R1. One photo, one component.** `HassUI/Common/HouseBackdrop.swift`: `HouseBackdropStore` loads `<server>/local/images/house.webp` through `ImagePipeline` ([image-cache](image-cache.md): disk cache, revalidation, swaps in a changed photo, decoded at up to 2560 px) and keeps the finished wallpaper (R8). `AppSession` creates the store at sign-in, before the first signed-in frame, so a relaunch draws the cached render on its first frame (AGENTS.md "No reload on reopen"). Renders are named by the recipe (`BackdropRenderSpec.recipe`, now `r3`; bump it whenever the look changes so an old render never shows under a new look) and live in `Caches/HouseBackdrop`, the last eight kept.
- **R2. Every page, the same crop.** `pageChrome()` draws `HouseBackdrop` as the navigation container background. The photo fills the window (`backdropCanvas`, measured by `MainTabView`) and each page or split pane shows the part under its own frame, so pushes, tabs and splits all show one picture. The shift never goes negative (`HouseBackdropStyle.shift`): a page sliding out of a push has a negative window position, and shifting by it left a strip of plain base color on the edge still showing (a black flash).
- **R3. Modals have none.** Sheets keep the system sheet background.
- **R4. Pages are transparent.** `pageChrome()` hides list backgrounds (`scrollContentBackground(.hidden)`); a page never paints an opaque full-page background of its own.
- **R5. Always dark (direction G).** The signed-in shell sets `preferredColorScheme(.dark)`. The photo is magnified 1.06 (baked into the render) under a black scrim that runs 0.44 → 0.45 (28%) → 0.46 (50%) → 0.52 (75%) → 0.64 from top to bottom, over the base `#111723` (`HouseBackdropStyle`). The top half never drops below 0.44, because white titles and section headers sit there (0.18 measured 3.0:1 under a light iPhone sky). Measured on the house photo (worst white-text contrast per tenth of the screen, iPhone 17 Pro): the old stops (0.40 and 0.42) with the web-matched blur gave 4.34:1 in the 30 to 40% band; these stops give 4.96:1 at the default blur and 4.71:1 at 25%. Below about 15% blur small text can dip under 4.5:1 over the bright roofline (3.9:1 at 0%): the slider's footer says so, and every scrim stop is 0.12 denser with Increase Contrast. Gallery pages get the same backdrop from `ComponentGallery`. There is no light wash any more.
- **R6. Reduce Transparency** draws the plain base color with no photo (the Background Blur slider stays, and its section footer says why nothing changes). **Increase Contrast** makes every scrim stop 0.12 denser (`HouseBackdropStyle.increasedContrastBoost`).
- **R7. The canvas is the whole window.** `backdropCanvas` is measured by `MainTabView` from a view *inside* the safe area, as its size plus its safe-area insets (`BackdropCanvas.size`). Never measure it from an `ignoresSafeArea()` view: that measured the safe-area rect (402 x 778 on an iPhone 18 Pro) and the photo ended 96 pt short, so a band of plain base color showed under the tab bar and the home indicator on every tab root and pushed page (owner, 2026-10-16: "Blurred image does not take full phone space"). Every pixel of the window, corners and edge midpoints included, shows wallpaper. A new root that draws a backdrop reads `backdropCanvas`; it never measures the window itself.

- **R8. Blur is rendered once, in points, at the web's value.** The web blurs by `16px` and then scales the element by `1.05` (`ha-sfenton-react-dash@f5a7bf9:src/components/shell/AppShell.module.css:58-65`: `filter: blur(16px) saturate(1.04) brightness(0.76)`, `transform: scale(1.05)`, `inset: -24px`), so the blur on screen is 16.8 CSS px, which is 16.8 pt; CSS `blur()` and Core Image's `CIGaussianBlur.inputRadius` are both the Gaussian's standard deviation, and the render works in sRGB, not linear light, like CSS filters. The earlier native recipe blurred a 480 px copy with sigma 20 and the view stretched it to the window, so the blur on screen was `20 x (window / 480)` (58 pt on a 402 x 874 pt iPhone with the 1024 x 684 house photo, 3.4 times the web's). Now `BackdropRenderer` makes the whole wallpaper in one Core Image pass at the window's pixel size (`BackdropRenderSpec`: the photo covers the window, magnified 1.06, saturated 1.1 (direction G), blurred by `radius in points x pixels per point`, edges clamped so nothing goes transparent). Where the blur is 8 px or wider the render is made at a half to a quarter of the window's pixels (`divisor`, up to 4) and the view scales it up: a 402 x 874 pt iPhone at 3x renders 302 x 656 px at the default, not 1206 x 2622. The view only draws the bitmap and offsets it by the page's position (no live `.blur`, no scale effect); no material or blur sits on a scrolling surface. Renders run off the main thread, one at a time; changes that arrive while one runs (a slider drag, rotation, a resized window) collapse into one more render of the latest, after a 140 ms gap; the previous render stays on screen until the next is ready. Renders are cached in memory (the last four) and on disk (`backdrop-<recipe>-<photo digest>-<w>x<h>-d<density>-b<percent>.jpg`), so returning to a setting is instant and a relaunch at the same window finds its render already on screen and draws nothing new. The dim is unchanged: direction G's scrim (R5), which is darker than the web's `brightness(.76)` plus 0.08 to 0.42 gradient, because white titles sit on it.
- **R9. Background Blur setting.** Settings → Appearance → Background Blur is a 0 to 100% slider in steps of 5 (`PercentSliderRow`, the app's one slider-with-value row: one adjustable element, label "Background Blur", value "50 percent"), with Reset to Default under it. 100% is twice the web's radius (33.6 pt) so the default, 50%, is the web's 16.8 pt and sits in the middle (`BackdropBlur`). It persists in `UserDefaults` (`backdropBlurPercent`; missing or invalid is 50, out of range is clamped) and changing it re-renders live. The section header and the slider show wherever Settings does; the App Icon row below them only where the system can switch icons.

## Tests

`shiftHoldsNegativePositionsAtZero` (in `HouseBackdropTests`) guards the push flash; a UI journey sampled too few frames of the 0.35 s push to fail on the bug, so there is none.

`Apps/iOSUITests/WallpaperJourneyTests.swift` (iPhone, folded Duo, iPad portrait and landscape): launches with `FENTON_BACKDROP_FIXTURE=1` (debug builds: `HouseBackdropStore.session(for:)` serves a bright synthetic photo from a throwaway folder, so wallpaper is tellable from the plain base whatever the house photo is), samples the four corners and four edge midpoints of a screenshot, and fails on any as dark as the base. It covers every tab root, Quick Links, a room page, Food and Media, each at the top and scrolled to the bottom, and Home after backgrounding the app and returning.

`Tests/HassUITests/HouseBackdropTests.swift` (URL, the render's size, blur softens detail, the blur's width on screen equals the setting's radius in points (a Gaussian edge's 10 to 90% rise), no transparent edges, the photo digest, store-and-load, the latest pointer, pruning, one refresh per store, newest photo wins, the previous render stays up while a new one is made, a burst renders once, a new window or photo re-renders and the same one does not, G's scrim stops and Increase Contrast, `BackdropCanvas` adds the insets, the fixture photo) and `Tests/HassCoreTests/BackdropBlurTests.swift` (the web mapping, percent clamping and labels, persistence, `onChange`, Reset, the render spec's sizes, divisor and cache names).

`Apps/iOSUITests/BackdropBlurJourneyTests.swift` (iPhone, folded Duo, iPad; `FENTON_GALLERY=settings` with `FENTON_BACKDROP_FIXTURE=1`, `FENTON_BACKDROP_BLUR=<percent>` to start elsewhere, `FENTON_BACKDROP_PHOTO=<path>` for a real photo in screenshots): the slider's name, role and value, 44 pt targets, Reset, an AX5 relaunch, the accessibility audit, the wallpaper still rendering at 0, 50 and 100% and its edges following the setting, Reduce Transparency's footer. `tools/scrollcost-surfaces.txt` has a `[backdrop]` section (`--section backdrop`) that runs three pages with the wallpaper behind them.
