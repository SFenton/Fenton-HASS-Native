# Dynamic grid

> **What:** the responsive tile grid. **Read when:** laying out tiles, cameras or quick links.

- One layout: `DynamicGridLayout(minCellWidth:maxColumns:spacing:)`. Column count is `floor((width + spacing) / (minCellWidth + spacing))`, clamped to `1...maxColumns`; leftover width is shared equally by all tracks.
- An unbounded proposal (an `HStack` probing a `maxWidth: .infinity` child, as the Food hub's landscape columns do) is treated like no proposal; `DynamicGridMetrics` with an infinite width returns `maxColumns` instead of trapping on `Int(.infinity)`.
- Items may claim more tracks with `.gridSpan(_:)`. An item never spans more columns than exist.
- Incomplete final rows align leading; never stretch a lone item to full width (web UX contract).
- **Equal row heights** (`equalRowHeights: true`): every tile row takes the tallest tile's height in the whole grid, so one wrapping title (Master Bedroom Closet on an iPhone) lifts every tile; tiles fill it inside `.equalHeightTiles()` (sets `tileFillsHeight`, read by `GlassTileFace`). A heading placed with `.gridHeader()` takes a whole row and keeps its own height, so one grid can hold several titled runs that share a height. Recomputed on every layout pass (Dynamic Type, rotation). Used by the room overview sheets.
- **Four is a floor on iPad, never a cap** (plan Phase 4, decision 1, 2026-10-15). A full-width tile grid must reach at least 4 columns at iPad Pro 11 portrait (content about 802 pt), and keep growing with width. The standard 150 pt tile (`minCellWidth` 150, 12 pt spacing) fits 5 at 802 pt, so a grid with `maxColumns` 4 or more and that tile width qualifies; a `maxColumns` of 3 does not and is not used on a tile grid. Tile grids pass `maxColumns` 6 unless a design reason bounds them. Recorded exceptions: Chores quick links stop at 4 (five links, one of them two tracks wide, fill four tracks exactly); grids of wide cards (`minCellWidth` 240 and up: Admin tiles, sheet detail cards) keep 2 or 3.
- **Sections side by side count as columns.** Room pages and Media lay two (Media: up to three) 340 pt section columns across (`maxColumns` 2 and 3) with each section's tiles in its own grid, so iPad portrait shows 2 + 2 tiles across: four tiles in a row, with a clear title over each group. That already meets the floor; the pages stay sectioned (web `RoomGrid`, `DashboardViewPage.tsx:284`) rather than flattening to one four-column grid. In landscape the section grid takes 3 columns of 2 tiles.
- **Hinge-aware even columns.** `DynamicGridLayout` read through `callAsFunction` (so every `DynamicGridLayout(...) { }` call) reads `EnvironmentValues.windowHinge` (`HassDesign/WindowHingeEnvironment.swift`, published by the shell's `readsWindowHinge()`). While a hinge is set (the unfolded iPhone Duo) the column count rounds down to an even number, never below two: `DynamicGridMetrics(width:minCellWidth:maxColumns:spacing:evenColumns:)` and `DynamicGridMetrics.evenRounded(_:)` (5 becomes 4, 3 becomes 2; a single track stays single). Leftover width is shared by the even tracks, so tiles grow rather than leave a gap. The grid does not know where the fold falls; with the window's content offset by the system's trailing bar the midline may still cross a tile on a one-grid page, which is why Home splits into two real columns on the hinge (`HomeColumns`, [duo](../design/apple/duo.md)). Unit tests: `DynamicGridMetricsTests`, `DynamicGridHingeTests`.
- Camera grid: `minCellWidth` 160, `maxColumns` 4 (web `maxCellWidth={280} maxColumns={4}`).
