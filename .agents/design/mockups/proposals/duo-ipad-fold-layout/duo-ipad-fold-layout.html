Duo and iPad: lay out around the fold, and cap width

27 review findings share one cause: pages are sized for "a wide window", not for the regions the window really has. On the unfolded Duo in landscape the hinge band is 40.4 pt wide at x 455.3 to 495.7, and text, tiles and sheets land under it. On iPad landscape the same pages stretch past a readable width. Nothing is built; each section asks for a pick.

Key: hinge band 83 pt rail content under the hinge SplitPolicy pane. All frames are drawn to scale (0.5 pt per px) from the measured Duo and the 1194 × 834 iPad.

Cause and evidence

SplitPolicy.geometry(container:isRegularWidth:hinge:) already gives the leading pane, the band and the trailing pane, and readsWindowHinge() already reports the hinge. Of the pages in this bucket, only the tab view reads it. Findings cite: FoodHubScreen (equal halves, 24 pt gap), RecipesScreen and InventoryScreen (HStack of two halves), VacuumsScreen (dividerX(hingeMidX: nil)), VacuumSheet (proxy.size.width / 2), TodoPageView (one list, no split), SecurityScreen and ClimateScreen (centred 868 and 720 pt columns, no hinge read), DynamicGridLayout (even columns but a 24 pt gutter, narrower than the 40.4 pt band), RootView (login not under readsWindowHinge), MainTabView (UIDevice.userInterfaceIdiom), VacationComponents and RoomScreen (minCellWidth: 340 literal). The atlas notes that none of the hinge behaviour was confirmed on a physical device.

1 · Two-pane screens ignore the hinge (Food hub, Recipes, Inventory, Vacuums, Vacuum sheet)

One answer, no design choice: take the pane widths from SplitPolicy.geometry instead of halving. The trailing pane starts at 495.7 pt, not at the midpoint, and the leading pane ends at 455.3 pt. On iPad the band is 1 pt at the midpoint, so iPad is unchanged. Visible only on the Duo.

2 · Chore, grocery and to-do lists are one column across the hinge

Six list pages (chores, resident, unassigned, home-improvement, groceries, admin to-do) are a single full-width List, so row titles and the Edit glyphs sit across the band. OnDemandSplitStack exists for this and has no adopter.

3 · Centred columns and grids straddle the fold (Security, Climate, Room, Household admin and guests, Login)

These pages centre a fixed-width column (868, 840, 720, 460 pt) in the window, so where the fold lands depends on the column width. On Security the fold crosses the alarm card, the third door tile and the third camera. DynamicGridLayout already rounds to an even column count but keeps a 24 pt gutter.

Recommendation: A for grids (Security, Room, Household admin and guests): one shared change, every consumer inherits it. Single objects that cannot split (alarm card, climate dial, login form) move to the leading pane's centre when the hinge crosses their frame, and only then; elsewhere they stay centred. This is the smallest rule that keeps everything off the band.

4 · Readable width on iPad landscape

AlarmPanel caps at 560 pt in its sheet but is unbounded on the Security page: the four mode buttons stretch to about 280 pt each across 1160 pt. Proposal: cap it at 560 pt, centred or leading-aligned with the page's other content. Climate (720) and the grids already cap.

5 · Page sheets cross the hinge (Auto-reset, Presence overrides)

On the unfolded Duo, system page sheets are centred, so a 540 pt form spans the band. A SwiftUI sheet cannot be offset, only sized (presentationSizing, iOS 18) or given a clear band inside its own content.

Recommendation: A, a fold-aware inset in the sheet content (rows keep the 40.4 pt band empty, grouped sections break at the fold), because it needs no new presentation API. Option B (a form-width sheet that fits one pane) is cleaner but cannot be placed by the app, so it is not offered. This is the item most worth confirming on a physical Duo.

Recommendation

Take every section's recommended option: pane widths from SplitPolicy.geometry everywhere (1), chore lists adopt OnDemandSplitStack (2), grids use a band-wide gutter and single objects shift into the leading pane only when the hinge crosses them (3), a 560 pt cap on the page alarm card (4), a fold-aware inset in the two page sheets (5), and the three small items (6).

Files: SplitPolicy.swift (a helper returning the band to avoid), DynamicGridLayout.swift, the nine screens named above, TodoPageView.swift, MainTabView.swift, HeaderEdgeFade. Tests: unit tests for the geometry helper and the grid with a band; UX and accessibility journeys on Duo (unfolded landscape), iPad landscape and iPhone (unchanged) at default and AX5; mockups updated to the picked options.
