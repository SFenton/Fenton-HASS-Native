## Cross-cutting observations

1. **Sheets are the strongest area.** Every room-device and bedroom sheet states its height and iPad size through `hassSheet`, shows a grabber, pairs a circle-X Close with an opaque ground, and keeps one sheet at a time except the scope prompt. The recorded departures (Close as `.confirmationAction`, opaque ground, icon-only section bar, full height for tabbed or navigating sheets) all trace to 2026-10-13 and 2026-10-16 owner decisions.
2. **Drafts have no leave guard.** Three pushed editors (bed alarm, wake alarm, humidifier activity) keep a `@State` draft and discard it on Back; only the wake editor has a dirty flag, and it is used for Save, not for leaving.
3. **Accessibility gaps cluster in custom rows.** Where rows are hand-built (alarm rows with nested switches, a tapped combined element, notices that hide their glyph, day chips that differ by fill only) the gaps are structural; the shared `GlassTile`, `SegmentedChoice` and `ModalTabBar` are consistently sound (labels, values, traits, AX reflow, Reduce Transparency).
4. **Live-value cost is uneven.** The fan tile gates its clock and rounds its spoken value; the bed tiles tick every second unconditionally and the fan sheet's countdown speaks every second. The AGENTS.md main-thread invariant says to apply the fan's pattern to both.
5. **Duo handling is by size class, not idiom, but the fold itself is unproven.** `SplitPolicy`, `WindowMetrics` and even column counts keep unfolded portrait single-column and landscape split; no gutter is widened to the hinge band, and every Duo-unfolded sheet placement is still "system decides, check on a device".
